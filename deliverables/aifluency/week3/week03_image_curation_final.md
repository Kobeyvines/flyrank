## If I decide I need one piece of generated connective tissue

I'll use one locked prompt, reused verbatim, so anything I generate stays in one family instead
of drifting:

> Generate a [specific element]. Style: calm, restrained, audit-grade — this sits behind real
> data charts and must never compete with them for attention. Palette: near-black #1A1A1A,
> near-white #FAFAFA, one accent blue #2D5FE0 used sparingly if at all. No gradients, no
> glass/blur effects, no glossy highlights, no abstract "data flow" or "neural network"
> clichés. Flat, minimal, almost invisible at a glance. If in doubt, simpler and quieter than
> my first instinct.

## What makes me reject an image immediately

- Glassy, melted, or waxy highlights — the telltale AI-slop look
- A gradient used as decoration rather than to convey information
- Generic "abstract data/network" imagery that doesn't depict anything specific to my actual work
- Colors outside my locked palette, or the accent used as a dominant color instead of a sparing one
- Anything that would be the most colorful thing on its page — if it beats my real charts for attention, it's out regardless of how it looks alone
- Fake text, fake UI chrome, or fake data that could pass as a real screenshot
